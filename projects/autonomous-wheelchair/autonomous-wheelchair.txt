[details]
Our four-person capstone started with a practical question: how could a wheelchair navigate more safely while also making biomedical information available for remote monitoring? We explored an autonomous wheelchair concept around those two goals.
The project brought machine learning together with Raspberry Pi 5 and ESP32-based embedded hardware. One of the central challenges was thinking through how the software and hardware could work together as part of one assistive system.
We presented our technical work to supervisors and external evaluators. They described the capstone as technically sound with real-world applicability. That feedback encouraged us to explain both the engineering choices and the need the project was intended to address.
For me the project was also a chance to work through a complex idea with a team and explain it to other people. The illustrations on this page are concept visuals made for the portfolio rather than records of a finished build.
